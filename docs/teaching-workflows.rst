Teaching Workflows
==================

Purpose
-------

This page turns the TSA29 alpha package into classroom workflow guidance. The
teaching goal is not to present one correct harvest schedule. It is to help
students inspect a real timber-supply-style model, understand its provenance,
and explain tradeoffs with appropriate caveats.

Starting Point
--------------

Students should start from the published Patchworks package, not from a fresh
THLB or yield rebuild:

.. code-block:: text

   models/tsa29_patchworks_model/analysis/

Before class use, instructors or teaching assistants should confirm the
runtime payloads are materialized and that the package opens on the teaching
workstation.

First Outputs To Inspect
------------------------

Use these surfaces to orient students before they change any scenario:

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1

   * - Surface
     - Teaching Use
   * - ``product.HarvestedVolume.managed.Total.CC``
     - Main managed clearcut harvest-volume signal used in the accepted
       representative evidence.
   * - ``models/tsa29_patchworks_model/tracks/accounts.csv``
     - Account definitions generated by Matrix Builder.
   * - ``models/tsa29_patchworks_model/tracks/features.csv``
     - Feature signals available to Patchworks.
   * - ``models/tsa29_patchworks_model/tracks/treatments.csv``
     - Treatment definitions available in the alpha package.
   * - ``output/patchworks_tsa29_validated/fragments/``
     - Spatial fragment surface behind the model.
   * - ``plots/tipsy_vdyp_tsa29-*.png``
     - Accepted TIPSY-vs-VDYP comparison figures for yield discussion.

Exercise 1: Read The Release Boundary
-------------------------------------

Ask students to identify which artifacts are source contracts, generated model
outputs, launch surfaces, and validation evidence.

Expected discussion points:

- ``config/`` and ``metadata/`` explain assumptions and lineage;
- ``models/`` and ``output/`` carry generated Patchworks-facing outputs;
- ``evidence/`` records acceptance checks; and
- alpha release evidence is useful but not a substitute for final production
  validation.

Exercise 2: Interpret THLB Provenance
-------------------------------------

Have students compare the locked THLB ledger with the prose status reports.
They should explain why the current release uses the locked ledger for
cumulative area while still preserving reviewed and reconstructed reports for
method context.

Expected discussion points:

- strict, reviewed, and reconstructed surfaces answer different questions;
- aspatial bridges can be honest when exact public spatial data is not a
  defensible representation;
- cumulative area should come from the locked ledger; and
- old diagnostic notes can remain valuable without governing the current
  release.

Exercise 3: Compare Yield Families
----------------------------------

Use the accepted ``54``-plot TIPSY-vs-VDYP family under ``plots/``. Students
should choose several analysis units and describe how managed and natural
curve assumptions affect scenario interpretation.

Expected discussion points:

- natural-origin and treated-origin curves are provenance lanes, not the same
  thing as Patchworks managed/unmanaged treatment eligibility;
- sparse or smoothed curve choices should be reported when they influence an
  interpretation; and
- yield comparison figures are evidence for review, not a standalone policy
  decision.

Exercise 4: Reproduce The Representative Harvest Signal
-------------------------------------------------------

Use the recorded ``test01`` evidence as an orientation target. Students should
inspect the managed clearcut harvest-volume output and discuss why a result in
the recorded range is a sanity check rather than a proof of correctness.

Expected discussion points:

- the accepted evidence lands in a plausible mid-term public-discussion band;
- the package may still have caveats in THLB, yield, treatment, or constraint
  design;
- scenario outputs depend on the selected target and constraints; and
- new student scenarios should record their own assumptions.

Exercise 5: Explain A Caveat Honestly
--------------------------------------

Assign each group one caveat from :doc:`known-limitations`. Their task is to
explain how that caveat could affect a scenario result and what evidence would
be needed to reduce uncertainty.

Scenario Reporting Rules
------------------------

Student reports should:

- identify whether results came from the published package or a rebuilt
  package;
- state the launch surface, scenario target, and major constraints;
- distinguish current evidence from proposed extensions;
- cite the THLB ledger or evidence file used for important area/validation
  claims;
- avoid treating alpha release status as production readiness; and
- disclose any new assumptions added during the exercise.
